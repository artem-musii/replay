# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Drivers documenting a minor collision, claims-support staff, fleet managers, insurance intake teams, rental-vehicle support teams, and neutral mediators. They use REPLAY shortly after an incident or during structured follow-up, often with incomplete memories, approximate timing, photographs, and unresolved disagreement. Their primary job is to organize a factual, reviewable account without allowing assumptions to harden into facts.

## Product Purpose

REPLAY is a local-first shared black box for minor two-vehicle road incidents. A person and an AI agent edit one spatial, temporal, and provenance-aware case model. Success means the human can reconstruct the scene manually, correct the agent directly, preserve uncertainty through hypothesis branches, and export a neutral report whose substantive statements remain linked to claims or evidence.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exacting, humane. The product should create the confidence of a carefully kept evidence notebook, the spatial clarity of a map editor, and the restraint of a neutral mediator.

## Anti-references

- Generic admin dashboards with interchangeable metric cards.
- Police, surveillance, emergency, courtroom, or adversarial visual clichés.
- Neon AI styling, pervasive gradients, glass surfaces, fake 3D, and alarming red-heavy palettes.
- Chat-first products that hide state changes in prose.
- Interfaces that imply fault, certainty, forensic authority, or legal advice.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the difference visible: observation, memory, uncertainty, dispute, and inference must never collapse into one style or one sentence.
2. Share one inspectable model: factual UI and agent mutations use the same canonical command layer and durable activity; read/UI-only tool invocations remain visibly auditable in a separate session log. WebMCP cancellation before primary persistence records neither layer, while a post-save cancellation is compensated or surfaced as audited persistence failure.
3. Let geometry lead: the scene and timeline are the work surface; inspectors support them instead of competing with them.
4. Earn trust through restraint: neutral language, deterministic checks, explicit provenance, and reversible actions are more valuable than spectacle.
5. Design the demo as a real workflow: a judge should understand the problem in ten seconds and the WebMCP advantage in thirty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Every scene action needs an equivalent numeric or lane-based form, keyboard movement and rotation, non-color status cues, visible focus, semantic controls, reduced-motion support, accessible dialogs, and readable metadata at compact desktop densities.
